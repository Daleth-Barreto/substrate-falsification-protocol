\begin{abstract}
\noindent
When a population of spiking neurons is read out to control a simulated
body, ``the substrate computes'' is usually claimed from a decoded
error or a mutual-information value. We show that such claims are
unfalsifiable unless the substrate is swapped for a \emph{dead}
substrate that preserves the marginal statistics of the spike train.
We build a minimal embodied hub (2,000\% neurons, fixed sparse wiring,
one calibrated decision map) and run three gates: (B) substrate swap
under a fixed readout; (D) optimal linear readout of the \emph{same}
spikes; (E) memory-demanding tasks. The dead substratum retains exactly
zero task information under every readout (MI $\approx 0$, TE $= 0$,
$\rho \approx 0$, all seeds, all profiles). The living substrates do
not uniformly survive: LIF carries information through an optimal
linear readout ($\Delta\RMSE = +0.094$ vs.\ the mean-predictor floor),
whereas the apparent Izhikevich tracking seen under the fixed
calibrated readout is largely a \emph{readout-channel artifact}:
decoding the same spikes optimally leaves it indistinguishable from
the floor. We conclude that (i) negative controls for the \emph{material}
of a substrate are cheap and decisive; (ii) claims of embodiment must
be made against the exact-zero null, not against scoreboards; and
(iii) the open-loop diagnosis cannot certify memory, which must be
demonstrated in the closed loop.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and hypothesis}\label{sec:problem}
Take a fixed wiring $\mathbf{g},\mathbf{W},\mathbf{w}_r$ (input
projection $\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$, sparse recurrence
$\mathbf{W}$, readout projection $\mathbf{w}_r$), an input profile
$u(t)$ sampled to $N_{\mathrm{win}}$ control windows, and a decision map
$\hat{x}=\mathrm{decide}(\theta_k)$. Swapping only the \emph{material}--
the per-neuron dynamics--while keeping wiring and the readout identical
lets us ask: \emph{does the substrate carry the task signal, or does
the readout-on-wiring do all the work?}

Hypothesis under test:
\begin{quote}
Resistance to a \emph{dead null} (a substrate that fires with the same
marginal rates but is statistically independent of $u$) is the weakest
correct certificate that the substrate computes; the stronger claim
requires the correct information to survive an \emph{optimal linear
readout} of the same spikes, and--for memory--a closed-loop task.
\end{quote}

Everything below is minimal, seeded, and reproducible
(\S\ref{sec:repro}). The canonical closed-loop result of the parent
project is \RMSE $=\canRMSE$ for the neural substrate vs.\ visible
ablations with exact paired permutation $p_{\mathrm{1s}}=\canPone$,
$p_{\mathrm{2s}}=\canPtwo$ and Cohen $d_z=\canDzRnd$ (random-mask) /
$\canDzMask$ (mask 0.5); those numbers are owned by the tracking
session and cited here as read-only reference
(\S\ref{sec:canonical}).

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{Hub.}
$N=2000$ neurons, $N_{\mathrm{win}}=200$ control windows, $40$ integration
steps per window. Wiring is seeded sparse; $W$ uses the previous window;
$\mathbf{w}_r=\mathbf{g}$ (channel aligned with the input projection,
calibrated $k_{\mathrm{ro}}$ per substrate). The decision map is the
verbatim calibrated one used in the canonical tracking code. Five control
profiles ($\mathtt{baseline},\mathtt{multi\_step},\mathtt{sine},
\mathtt{descend},\mathtt{pulse}$), six seeds $\{1,7,13,29,55,91\}$.

\paragraph{Substrates.}
LIF (leaky integrate-and-fire, $\tau=40$ ms) and Izhikevich regular
spiking ($d=8$ adaptation), and the \emph{dead null} \texttt{Poisson}:
per-neuron Poisson rates matched to LIF's marginal histogram
(mean \SI{8.09}{spike\,s^{-1}} each), \emph{statistically independent}
of $u$. All three use identical wiring, bias, drive and readout
constants; only the dynamics differ.

\paragraph{Decoders (Gate D).}
On the \emph{same} recorded spike trains:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{canon}--the fixed calibrated channel $k_{\mathrm{ro}}
\mathbf{w}_r\!\cdot(\text{rates})$ + \texttt{decide};
\item \textbf{ridge}--optimal L2-regularized linear readout of the
population counts, fit in nested train/val/test windows (interleaved
splits keep every phase in every fold), solved via the Woodbury
identity with 13 lambdas in $[10^{-5},10]$ and bias via centering;
verified against a direct solve (max diff $<4\times10^{-10}$);
\item \textbf{meanpd}--constant-predictor floor
($\mathrm{var}$-around-run-mean);
\item \textbf{passthru}--$0.75u$ directly ($\RMSE=0$ by construction,
ceiling).
\end{enumerate}
``carried info'' $=$ $\RMSE_{\mathrm{meanpd}}-\RMSE_{\mathrm{ridge}}$.

\paragraph{Memory (Gate E).}
Three history-dependent tasks (\texttt{lag}: 4-window delayed
reference; \texttt{integ}: running integral smoothed over 8 windows;
\texttt{stair}: staircase level switch) decoded by the ridge of the
present-window spikes vs.\ an 8-tap FIR over the input only. The dead
null here is the same Poisson substrate; ``transposing past into
present spikes'' would show as a positive \emph{carried} for the neural
substrates.

% Table 2 above
Table~2 (pooled over the five profiles, six seeds) shows the key
result. The ridge readout of \emph{the same spikes}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{LIF}: drops \RMSE{} to 0.055 -- the fixed channel had
left information on the table (canon-to-ridge gap $+0.113$), and the
spikes genuinely carry the task ($\MI$ significant $0.80$ fraction,
carried $+0.094$);
\item \textbf{Izhikevich}: the fixed-channel ``tracking'' does \emph{not}
persist -- ridge collapses to 0.157 (approx. mean-predictor)
(0.149), carried $\approx 0$ ($-0.008$). The Gate~B IZH gap was
largely a \emph{readout-channel artifact}: $\mathbf{w}_r=\mathbf{g}$ is
pre-aligned with the input projection. An ablation that randomizes
$\mathbf{w}_r$ (verify/verify\_wr.py) collapses \emph{both} living
substrates to the null floor, confirming the channel, not the substrate
material, held the Gate~B signal. IZH retains only weak MI
($0.25$; sig.\ in $23/30$ runs) under the optimal readout.
\item \textbf{Dead null}: ridge 0.158 $\approx$ floor, MI $\approx 0$
(frac.\ $0.03$), \TE{} exactly $0$ -- the exact-zero null survives the
optimal linear decoder.
\end{itemize}
Recurrence ablation (nohub) leaves canon \RMSE{} unchanged to
$<0.001$; the mini is effectively feed-forward.

Negative result. Under memory demand the dead null stays dead
(carried $-0.006$), but \emph{neither} LIF (carried $-0.13$) nor
Izhikevich ($-0.06$) transposes the past into present-window spikes;
an 8-tap FIR over the input solves the linearly-recoverable tasks
(carried $+0.14\ldots+0.15$). The mini therefore cannot certify
substrate memory. This is consistent with a feed-forward diagnostic;
functional memory claims must be made in the closed loop
(\S\ref{sec:canonical}).

\subsection{Verification (robustness, not bugs)}\label{sec:verify}
Three independent checks confirm the above is not an implementation
artifact (\path{sandbox/verify/}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \texttt{verify\_ridge.py}--Woodbury ridge equals a direct
least-squares solve (max $|\Delta|<4\times10^{-10}$), $\lambda$ grid
wide, bias centered: the optimal linear readout numbers are exactly the
solver's;
\item \texttt{verify\_null.py}--Poisson dead null on two seeds outside the
calibrated family $\{103,201\}$: $\MI=0$, $\TE=0$, $\rho=0$, exact;
\item \texttt{verify\_wr.py}--randomizing $\mathbf{w}_r$ collapses LIF and
IZH to the floor while the null is unaffected, isolating the aligned
channel as the carrier of the Gate~B signal.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What survives.}
The dead-source null is not just a benchmark--it is the \emph{weakest
honest certificate}: a substrate indistinguishable from independent
Poisson firing is not computing the task, on any readout we tried,
including the optimal linear one and memory demands. We believe this
``matched dead-substrate null + decoder-invariance'' protocol is the
correct unit to slot in next to decoder ablations in the embodied-AI
literature (cf.\ \cite{kagan2022dishbrain,balci2023neurons,millar2025}). 

\paragraph{What does not.}
The Izhikevich result is the sharp consequence: a substrate can look
tracked under an \emph{aligned} readout yet carry almost nothing to an
optimal decoder; conversely LIF carries a real rate code. Readout
choice is therefore a \emph{substantive} modeling decision, not a
detail. Overselling ``intelligence on a dish'' (or in a loop) without
these controls is exactly the pattern that recent commentary warns
against \cite{balci2023neurons}; our protocol makes the claim
falsifiable.

\paragraph{Limits.}
The mini is open-loop and feed-forward; history-dependent inference is
explicitly \emph{not} certified here (Gate E negative). A closed-loop
functional test (perturbation/memory) is required before any ``the
substrate computes for the robot'' claim; that is the canonical battery
cited in \S\ref{sec:canonical} and its successors. Only LIF--of the
three \emph{materials}--survives the readout controls as a carrier of
task information, and only for the rate-code tasks.
